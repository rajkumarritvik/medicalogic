Debunked Viral Claims

These claims were checked by our research team against PubMed. Show them to your students as ready-made examples of how a fact-check works.

“Clean your liver in just one night. This detox drink saved my liver!”

This claim is…

Not supported by evidence

⋮
Scientific summary
a. What’s true

Your liver already filters out and removes unwanted substances on its own.

b. What’s overstated

The liver doesn’t need an outside “cleanse,” and no drink has been shown to clean it. Some detox supplements have even been linked to liver damage.

Sources and audit trail

PubMed · PMID: 38062980 · Review · 2023 (Direct)Hepatotoxicity due to dietary supplements: state-of-the-art, gaps and perspectives

PubMed · PMID: 1749210 · Review · 1991 (Indirect)Detoxification pathways in the liver

What to do instead

Keep a healthy lifestyle: eat balanced meals, drink enough water, exercise, and maintain a healthy weight. Learn more

“Whiten your teeth in 2 minutes at home with activated charcoal!”

This claim is…

Not supported by evidence

⋮
Scientific summary
a. What’s true

Charcoal can scrub off some surface stains from coffee, tea, or food.

b. What’s overstated

It doesn’t whiten teeth the way professional treatments do, and its rough texture can wear down enamel with regular use.

Sources and audit trail

PubMed · PMID: 36183933 · Systematic review · 2023 (Direct)Effectiveness and abrasiveness of activated charcoal as a whitening agent: A systematic review of in vitro studies

PubMed · PMID: 28599961 · Review · 2017 (Direct)Charcoal and charcoal-based dentifrices: A literature review

What to do instead

Ask your dentist about professional whitening, and avoid DIY remedies such as charcoal or baking soda. Learn more

“I found an antibiotic that works very well for colds!”

This claim is…

Not supported by evidence

⋮
Scientific summary
a. What’s true

Antibiotics are powerful medicines made to treat bacterial infections.

b. What’s overstated

Colds and the flu are caused by viruses, which antibiotics can’t kill. Taking them anyway adds side effects without helping you recover.

Sources and audit trail

PubMed · PMID: 41277585 · Meta-analysis · 2025 (Direct)Antibiotics for the common cold and acute purulent rhinitis

PubMed · PMID: 10796517 · Systematic review · 2000 (Direct)Antibiotics for the common cold

What to do instead

If you have a cold, see a doctor to manage your symptoms, and avoid antibiotics, since they don’t work on viruses. Learn more

“Major life hack: put toothpaste on burns. It works like magic!”

This claim is…

Not supported by evidence

⋮
Scientific summary
a. What’s true

The menthol in toothpaste can feel briefly cool on the skin.

b. What’s overstated

Toothpaste doesn’t heal burns and isn’t a recommended first-aid step.

Sources and audit trail

PubMed · PMID: 37405866 · Systematic review · 2023 (Indirect)Review of Traditional First Aid for Burn Injuries in the 21st Century

PubMed · PMID: 37885545 · Survey study · 2023 (Direct)Perception, Awareness, and Practices Related to Burn First Aid Among the General Population in Qassim Region, Saudi Arabia

What to do instead

For a minor burn, cool it under running water, apply a burn ointment, and don’t pop blisters. For a deep burn, go to the hospital. Learn more
